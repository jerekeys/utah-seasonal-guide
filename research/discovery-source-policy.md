# Discovery source policy

Updated October 14, 2026

Discovery platforms produce leads, not publish-ready records. Every candidate still receives an editorial and detail-verification pass before it appears on Utah Every Season.

## Source tiers

### Strong discovery sources

- Official organizer, venue, city, county, library, park, museum, university and cultural-organization calendars
- Official ticket sellers and registration pages
- KSL Events, NowPlayingUtah, Visit Utah and destination calendars
- Local reporting and curated seasonal guides that identify specific public events

These sources may establish that an event exists, but dates, hours, prices, age limits, accessibility and pet policies should still be checked against the organizer, venue or ticket flow when one is available.

### Broad discovery sources

- Eventbrite
- Eventeny
- Meetup
- Facebook Events
- AllEvents and similar aggregators

These are useful for recall and for finding small community events. They require stronger duplicate, privacy and organizer checks.

## Public-event gate

A candidate may be published only when all of the following are true:

1. It is intentionally offered to the public or to a clearly described public-facing community.
2. The organizer, venue or sponsoring organization can be identified.
3. A reader can understand how to attend without relying on a personal invitation or private relationship.
4. The listing does not expose a private residential gathering, personal contact information or a hidden group location.
5. The event fits the guide's editorial scope and has enough current practical detail to be useful.

Events may be free, ticketed, RSVP-based or membership-based. A public LGBTQ+ gathering, faith-community observance or affinity-group event is not treated as private merely because it serves a particular community.

## Platform-specific rules

### KSL Events

Use as a high-volume statewide discovery source. Prefer festivals, cultural events, seasonal programming, community gatherings, distinctive performances, family events and unusual local experiences. Routine classes, ordinary touring concerts and daily museum entries need a stronger seasonal, cultural or editorial reason to be included. Follow KSL's link to the organizer or venue and verify the practical details there.

### Meetup

Publish only when the event is publicly discoverable and the group or organizer has an established public identity. Do not publish an event whose description or location is hidden behind membership. Recurring social meetups may qualify when they are clearly open to newcomers, have a public venue and fit a community or campaign need; a casual gathering at a member's home does not.

### Facebook Events

Prefer public events hosted by an established Facebook Page for an organization, venue, business or public program. A public event created from an individual's profile requires corroboration from the venue, ticket seller or organization. Exclude private, friends-only and private-group events, and do not surface residential addresses unless the address belongs to an established public attraction or business that is openly promoting the event.

### Eventeny and AllEvents

Use for discovery, then verify with the organizer or venue. Aggregated duplicates, copied descriptions and outdated editions must not be treated as independent confirmation.

## Verification depth

- Open ticket and registration links far enough to capture required fees and ticket tiers.
- Read dates, hours and price information embedded in official posters and images.
- Look for organizer- or venue-published accessibility details; do not infer accommodations from the type of venue.
- Apply every relevant holiday, season, cultural, community, age, activity and pet flag rather than forcing one primary category.
- Use current-year language and dates together. Stale marketing language does not by itself disqualify a clearly current event.
- Keep uncertain leads in research output rather than converting uncertainty into confident public copy.

## Personal-party safeguards

Automatically exclude candidates with signals such as invitation-only language, private-group access, an unnamed host, a residential address without an established public attraction, instructions to direct-message a person for the address, or no public attendance path. Borderline cases require organizer or venue corroboration before publication.
